# Reflection

```
Essence ⧁ Shine ⟹ Identity

Essence ⧀ Shine ⟹ Difference

Identity ⧁ Difference ⟹ Positive

Identity ⧀ Difference ⟹ Negative

Positive ⧁ Negative ⟹ Contradiction

Positive ⧀ Negative ⟹ Contradiction

Positive ⟲ Negative ⟹ Ground
```

As reflection describes how one concept changes into another, then the essence between concepts is what remains constant. This always has to be the case, because if the essence changed in the act of reflection, the concepts would lose their meaning. So essence is a reflection into itself, meaning that what is essential is identical with itself. This gives us the Law of Identity, A=A.

This result should seem unsatisfying at first glance, since the assertion of self-identity (A=A) is not the same thing as truth. The Law of Identity necessarily excludes all difference for the sake of precision, whereas truth isthe whole, the systematic totality of true propositions. The basis of all knowledge must rest on the latter because it accounts for the diversity of, and differences between, concepts that exist in reality. If the Law of Identity was taken as a basis, it would be impossible to introduce new information. For instance, if for some reason we wanted to learn about baking pies from a book that contained the one and only sentence, “A pie is a pie”, we might as well have not learned anything at all.

If identity is expressed as the Law of Identity, then difference must have its corresponding law. The expression which captures is Leibniz's Identity of Indiscernables, in which states that no two objects have all their properties in common.

Identity cannot exist without difference and difference cannot exist without identity. They constitute inseparable, essential moments which pervade all that exists. Their unity exists only in their opposition and without it they would be indistinguishable. (e.g. The assertion that A is identical to A and different from B presupposes that A opposes, or is not equal to, B) 

Whereas difference enumerates the dissimilarities between two concepts, opposition isolates a concept with its exact opposite. Two concepts that are absolutely different are similar in that only they oppose each other and nothing else. Hence, the two concepts form ends of the same pole, a positive and negative. 

Any two concepts that form positive-negative pair are dual to each other. Here, we have introduced a new type of relation that finally resolves the problems we encountered earlier with identity and contradiction. Identity's relation of equality (=) was too strict, both sides of the equation were indistinguishable and introduced no new information. Contradiction, on the other hand, showed that it was impossible for equality to assert absolute difference and not erase itself in the process. Duality mediates between the two as it allows us to describe absolutely different concepts without contradiction. 

So contradiction must “fall to its ground”, which is to say that when contradictions emerge, there is always an underlying basis or reason explains why the contradiction appeared in the first place.
