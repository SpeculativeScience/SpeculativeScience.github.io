## Pure Quantity

```
Discrete ( Continuous )
[ Discrete → Continuous ] ⟹ Unit
[ Discrete ← Continuous ] ⟹ Amount
[ Discrete ⇄ Continuous ] ⟹ Number
```

Continuity and discreteness are inseperable moments of determinate quantity. This quantitative determinateness is the Quanta, a specific magnitude which has distinguished itself from magnitude as such by excluding itself from all other specific magnitudes.

## Quantum

```
Unit ( Amount ) = Number
Amount of Units ⟹ Extensive Magnitude
Unit of Amount ⟹ Intensive Magnitude
Number ⟹ Qualitative Something (x)

Extensive Infinite Progress
x → x^2 → x^3 → … ⟹ ∞  

Intensive Infinite Progress
x → x^-2 → x^-3 → … ⟹ 1/∞ ⟹ dx
```

A Quanta can either be thought of either as an Amount of something or by itself as a Unit. An Amount is the Discrete Magnitude of a Quanta and a Unit is its Continuous Magnitude. That which is both an Amount and a Unit is a Number, the signifier which represents the Quanta.

The duality between the unit and the amount is mostly self-explanatory. An amount is a multiplicity of units and a unit is a singular amount. The mediation between the two is the number, which consists of both a unit and an amount. 

Take the number 12 for example. We can think of 12 either as an amount of units (e.g. 12 units of 1) or as a unit of amount (e.g. 12 as 1 unit), but both are equal to same number. This can be illustrated more clearly for divisors of numbers. In the case of 12, by defining its unit of amount as 6, its corresponding amount of units is 2 (6x2 = 12).

An Extensive Magnitude is a Number which signifies the cardinality (size) of an Amount, whereas an Intensive Magnitude is a Number which signifies the ordinality (order) of a Unit. 

An example of an Extensive Magnitude would be the mass of an object, since mass depends on the Amount of matter. By contrast, an object’s pressure would be its Intensive Magnitude, since pressure is an ordered scale of Units which does not quantify the Amount of matter in a space.

An Intensive Magnitude can have a value at a point in space, whereas an Extensive Magnitude cannot. For instance, if I were to give you a metal plate and ask you what the temperature (intensive) was at at a specific point, the question has an exact answer. However, if I were to ask what the mass of the metal plate was at the same point, my question wouldn’t make much sense since mass is an Amount, not a numerical value at a point. 

Since an Extensive Magnitude is an Amount of Units and Intensive Magnitude a Unit of Amount, the two are necessarily self-related in that they are both moments of finite quantity. The general idea of a finite quantity is something which can change quantitatively, but remain what it is qualitatively. This role is fulfilled by the algebraic variable (x), a Quantitative Something which can represent any Number. 

The quantified difference between one Quantitative Something [x0] and its other [x] is its Quantitative Change (Δx).

We make this more explicit by way of example: 
Take r to be a real number. We have two ways of interpreting r, as a magnitude. One way is to take it to be an element of the real line. Another way is to take r to represent the size/cardinality of the interval [0, r]. This interval is extensive, in the sense that it refers to the total collection of its members, but not "itself" (the analogue to this idea in set theory is called a fusion). However, if you just take the upper boundary of this interval, you have isolated its intensive magnitude which refers to itself by excluding itself from other numbers (and this very act of exclusion determines what it is!)

Quantitative Change must either move towards the infinitely small or infinitely great. The infinitely small Quantitative Change that moves towards, but never reaches 0, is the Infinitesimal. The infinitely great Quantitative Change that moves towards, but never reaches −∞ or +∞, is the Infinite Series. This negation process is negated by the Ratio, a quantitative relation of two Numbers that are self-external to each other (and not endlessly towards an indefinite beyond).
