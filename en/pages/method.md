# The Speculative Method

The speculative method is divided into three parts – The Understanding, Dialectical Reason and Speculative Reason.

## The Understanding (Immediacy)

The Understanding examines conceptual difference, determines what each concept before it has in common and simplifies these determinations into an abstract universal. It takes content and translates it into form. This newly formed concept is the totality of what came before *as* an immediacy. However, once the Understanding translates information to a one-sided proposition, it already implies the existence of its negation.

## Dialectical Reason (Mediation)

Dialectical Reason counters what the Understanding previously proposed by recovering the negative that the Understanding did not account for. As such, the immediacy and its negative are now mediated. At first it would seem that that the immediate and its negative are expressly different, since the immediate is the immediate and the negative is the negative. The two are externally opposed and are thereby distinguishable, or determinate. However, for the relationship between two things to be dialectical, the difference between them must be shown to be necessarily, rather than contingently opposed. For instance, a tractor and a sofa are opposed because a tractor is not a sofa and a sofa is not a tractor. However, the existence of a tractor does not categorically imply the existence of sofas nor is the reverse true. The two concepts are only externally opposed, but are not internally contradictory since were brought together arbitrarily by thought (more specifically, a kind of thinking called external reflection). By contrast, the concepts of being ↔ nothing, reality ↔ negation, something ↔other, (etc.) all constitute dialectical moments because the criterion for biconditionality is satisfied – if the positing of one side implies the other side and vice versa.

## Speculative Reason (Self-Mediation)

Speculative Reason recollects the complete movement that took place and identifies a new, successive concept that encompasses the preceding two. In the dialectical moment, the positive and negative sides excluded their intersection to distinguish themselves from each other. Ironically, their intersection is what they had in common, so the activity of distinction showed that both sides were one and the same, bringing them into contradiction (negative). Though each immediacy is mediated by its other (negative), the mediation that unifies the extremes remains what it is (positive). This mediation is subsumed by within another concept of which it is part. So speculative reason is not a “synthesis” of two opposites into a simplex, but complex of two opposites that are overgrasped by a third. This new content or generated concept is unified by the Understanding, establishing a new form or regulative pattern for advancing discourse.

## A Syntactic Description

What are called "conceptual moments" are (co)modalities on a category C: an idempotent operator that maps an object X of C to another object ⃝X which is the aspect of X under ⃝, for which there exists another operator under the image of X: ⃝X -> X or X -> ●X. 

The kind of mappings that are relevant here are embeddings and projections. An embedding is generalization of an onto / surjective function and a projection is a generalization of the into / injective function. With this in mind, we describe syntax of the speculative method as follows:

Since the modalities are idempotent, any objects that are invariant under said (co)modality forms its own sub-category, which are designated C ⃝ and C●. The two sub-categories C ⃝ and C● are each projected out of C, but correspondingly, both sub-categories share a common embedding into C. Moreover, the embedding composes with both projections to give isomorphisms, which indicates that the two sub-categories are identical and opposite to each other.

However, once the two sub-categories C ⃝ and C● have been projected out, there exists another category C' such that C ⃝ and C● are embedded into C', but correspondingly, both sub-categories share a common projection out of C'. The projection composes with both embeddings to give isomorphisms, indicating identical and opposite to each other, but they have been sublated (canceled and preserved) within C'.

Putting this all together, we have the three stages or moments of the speculative method. The Understanding takes a concept C and projects out C ⃝ an attempt to make C more determinate (making explicit what was implicit). Dialectical Reason opposes C ⃝ with C●, showing that C ⃝ was a one-sided determination that excluded C●, but in so doing, C ⃝ and C● ended up being the same (by their common embedding). Speculative Reason recollects the two by embedding C ⃝ and C● into a new category C' which sublates (preserves and negates) the unity of opposites.

Recollecting what has been explicated so far, for philosophy to be thinking in and for itself, it cannot presuppose a beginning or foundation that is unproven, otherwise it is externally determined by another and hence not self-coherent. Thought has to be able to generate and derive its form and content all on its own. As we have seen, the activity which satisfies this criterion is the speculative method.
