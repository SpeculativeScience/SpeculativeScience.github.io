## Specific Quantity

```
Immediate Measure:
Quantity is Quality

Mediated Measure:
Quantity is not Quality | Quality is not Quantity

Specifying Measure (The Measuring):
Quantity1 is Quality1  ⟹ Specifying Measure 

Specified Measure (The Measured):
Quantity2 is Quality2 ⟹ Specified Measure 

Specified Measure ⇄ Specifying Measure ⟹ Ratio of Measures
```

The Ratio of Powers, or binary relation, establishes an immediate measure as relating a quantity with a quality. The immediate measure becomes mediated measure when the quality remains the same after quantitative change, and so the two measures form an infimum and supremum on a line. This line is a poset, or Rule for measurement.

The Rule itself is now the Unit, a dichotomy that partitions an Amount into distinct quantities. Every particular partition is itself new quality that distinguishes itself from the other partitions. It is an external standard for measurement that consists of an upper bound and a lower bound. The Rule exists outside from and acts on what is measured, the Measure-Amount.

Whenever a measure is conceived of as a rule, it is posited externally to what is measured. The act of measuring, then, is an act of comparison between the measure and the measured. The measure (rule or specified measure) describes the relationship between quantities whereas the measured (specifying measure) captures the relationship between qualities. The mediation and unity between the two is a measure relation, or ratio of measures. 

## Real Measure

```
Combination of Measures:
Quantity1 of Quality1 is Quantity2 of Quality2

Series of Measures:
Quantity1 of Quality1 is Quantity2 of Quality2 is Quantity3 of Quality3 … 

Elective Affinity:
Quality1 is ( Quantity1 ⋆ Quantity2 → Quantity3 )

Substrate:
Quality1 ⟹ ( Quality1, Quality2, Quality3 … ) 

Nodal Line:
( Quantity1 ⋆ Quantity2 → Quantity3 ) ⟹ ( Quality1, Quality2, Quality3 … )

Measureless:
( Quality1, Quality2, Quality3 … ) is ( Quality1, Quality2, Quality3 … )
```

The Specified Measure and Specifying Measure are brought in combination with each other. The Specified Measure is quantifying thing, like a graduated cylinder or thermometer, whereas the Specifying Measure is the thing quantified, like the volume of some amount of water or the temperature of a room. However, the choice of a standardized Specified Measure is arbitrary and conventional - you can quantitatively relate any two things as long as the two are qualitatively overlap, like having volume or temperature that can be calculated. Since the choice of Specified Measure is entirely at the discretion of the measurer, so what results a series of measures that runs on indefinitely, unable to be Specifying Measure for what it is (Measureless). However, the quantities between each series of measures have determinate qualitative relationships precisely because the relation of quantities themselves are qualitatively invariant. This is an Elective Affinity, wherein you have a group-structure that brings together relationships / symmetries between a series of measures (e.g. the four seasons form a cyclic group, acid-base interactions form a klein-four group, a deck of cards forms a permutation group, so do the roots of polynomials, etc.)

The enriched view of Measure we have developed is a series of affinities which stand in contrast to their excluded neutralities. To move from one affinity to the next is to make a quantitative leap across what is neutral. Hence, each affinity is a “node” on a line of neutralities. 

The external measure's relationship to its internal measure is a qualitative one. However, the series of elective affinities it generates is quantitative. This arrangement displays the continuous progression from one affinity to another. The change from one affinity to another is a change in the measure relation from one to another. These differences in affinity causes the measure relation to be self-repelling, forming a nodal line of measure relations wherein each node signifies a change in quality and each line signifies a change in quantity.

## Becoming of Essence

```
Measureless ( Measure1, Measure2, Measure3 … )

Absolute Indifference ⧁ Inverse Ratio of Factors 

Essence ⧁ Shine
```

Measurements are determined and imposed by an external measurer on the phenomenon that is measured, not inherent to the phenomenon itself. The essence of the phenomenon is that it is measureless, it is mediated by itself because only it is adequate to its own description. The failure of the empirical method lies in its very confusion of the phenomenon being measured as the measure itself. In other words, the measure is true because it is false.
