# Measure

As being determined in and for itself, which has the aspect of its existence in indifferent quantity, measure is not yet true infinity. For the ideal determinacy that constitutes its inner, immanent being, in contrast to its outer indifferent being, is not yet the completeness of determinacies, the infinite determination of being, but only one ideal determinacy opposed to another ideal determinacy. Quality is thus reestablished in measure, but quality now stands opposed to quantity. The quality as ideal determinacy has attained a certain infinite being-for-itself that still persists in finitude because it has other ideal determinacies alongside it. Only the dialectical movement of measure returns to true infinity since, first, we will have to consider the immediate measure or measurement as the struggle between quality existing in-and-for-itself and the external indifference of quantity. But since the result of this struggle will be the dissolution of this opposition in the mediated measure or measure relation, we secondly must present the relationship of qualitative quantities, as ideal determinations, to one another. Finally, the qualitative quantities having sunk into indifference and externality as the true infinity of being, will form the transition to essence as the indifference to being.


## Measurement

```
[ Quantity → Quality ] ⟹ Specifying Measure

[ Quantity ← Quality ] ⟹ Specified Measure

[ Quantity ⇄ Quality ] ⟹ Measure Relation
```

As self-referential externality, which at the same time bears this externality within itself, measure is a quantitative quality. While quality determines quantity, thus negating its externality, it also simultaneously leaves it free, since the quality does not completely determine it. The quantity determined by quality is the measurement or rule, whereas the quantity left free is the remainder or the exception to the rule. At first glance, measure appears to be something purely quantitative due to the role that quantity plays in measurement. However, units of length, such as feet or inches, are originally qualitative determinates, namely a human foot, the joint of the thumb, etc. Such qualities usually have a length that constitutes the rule, although variations in this length also occur, which must be considered exceptions. 

However, even variability is not merely an exception since the rule is not a completely fixed quantity, but allows the indifference of quantity a margin of error within certain limits. Hence it also happens that among different choices of measure that the quality accepted as the rule also varies quantitatively, even if the margin of error is insignificant. Furthermore, it is the margins that determine the rule and only when this margin is exceeded does the exception begin.

The fact that the rule is the qualitative quantity cannot be altered even by exceptions to it, firstly because the latter is by definition rare and secondly because even it only barely deviates from the common quantity, that the exceptions nevertheless confirm the rule. However, since measure, as a rule, only tolerates the indifference of quantity to a certain degree, its indifference and externality are thereby abolished and has become something determined in-and-for-itself. If quantity with its indifferent externality not only transcends the rule to become the exception, but even beyond it, it thereby becomes that which determines quality. Hence a grain does not make a pile of sand and a plucked hair does not make a bald head, until the quality finally changes past the addition or removal of a certain amount of units. Likewise, vice is an excessive or deficient satisfaction of a drive, which brought back to the mean as the correct measure of it, becomes a virtue.

Because what drives quantitative change is qualitative change, quantity always remains the passive and in the background. Quantity does not become equal to quality, but in being changed by quantity, the latter refers to another quality, which determines the quantity in a different way from the first. Thus quality and quantity do not relate to each other directly, but through another measure where each side of the relation are themselves measures that have quantity and quality as their moments.


## Measure Relation

```
[ Specifying Measure → Specified Measure ] ⟹ Combination of Measures

[ Specifying Measure ← Specified Measure ] ⟹ Series of Measures

[ Specifying Measure ⇄ Specified Measure ] ⟹ Elective Affinity

[ Affinity of Measure → Neutrality of Measure ] ⟹ Nodes

[ Affinity of Measure ← Neutrality of Measure ] ⟹ Line

[ Affinity of Measure ⇄ Neutrality of Measure ] ⟹ Nodal Line

Measureless ( Nodal Line )
```

In the relationship between independent measures, quality and quantity do not determine each other alternately, rather the immanent determinations act on each other through their externalities. What gives quantity this power to produce a qualitative determinateness is the gradualness of transition. Where we see actual measures in conflict with each other, the gradual change produced by the changed quantity is merely the outer shell of the sudden transformation of one quality into the next. 

Specified measure and specifying measure are the two sides of the measure relation. The specified measure is quantifying quality, e.g. a graduated cylinder or thermometer, whereas the specifying measure is the quality quantified, e.g. the volume of an amount of water or the temperature of a room. However arbitrary the choice of specified measure is, you can quantitatively relate any two things as long as the two are qualitatively overlap. Since the choice of specified measure is entirely at the discretion of the measurer, a series of measures may run on indefinitely.

The quantities between each series of measures have determinate qualitative relationships precisely because the relation of quantities themselves are qualitatively invariant. This is an elective affinity or closed series of measures (e.g. the four seasons, acid-base interactions, suits of playing cards, roots of polynomials, the intervals within an octave, etc.). Elective affinity displays a continuous progression from one measure to another. It is a series of measures that stands in contrast to the neutral line it occupies. To move from one measure to the next is to make a quantitative leap across degrees. However, this relationship of one measure to another does not remain, but the second measure is overcome by a third and so on, forming a nodal line of measure relations.

After quantity externalizes a new quality up to a certain degree of its gradual change, the sudden leap occurs again to which another gradual progression gives way, so that both modes of change are in constant alternation. This alternation of instantaneous and gradual change, which most measure relations exhibit, cannot continue indefinitely. For since measure is only a relative being-in-and-for-itself, it cannot yet escape the finitude of quantity. But because quantity endlessly progresses to infinity, it will be able to surpass any determinacy of measure, thereby transcending measure into what is without measure or measureless.


## Indifference

```
Measureless ( Nodal Line )

Indifference ⧁ Ratio of Factors 

Essence ⧁ Shine
```

The indeterminacy of the measureless is, once again, only a one-sidedness. But the measureless is not merely indeterminate in that it contains all determinations of being within itself. Since every inner determination appears in the form of a merely external difference, we have the indifference of all these aspects of being, they are all sublated and have become dependent moments. At the same time, however, they remain firmly distinct from each other as independent moments. This contradiction can only be resolved through the concept of true infinity, where determinations are independent from each other while being dependent emanations of the infinite.

This ideality is no longer an ideal determination, as in measure, but posits all being as ideal, so that it is no longer contained in it immediately, but only as something real insofar as it is posited by the self-determination of the infinite. This infinite ideality is essence.
