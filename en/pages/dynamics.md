# Dynamics

## Force and Velocity (Pure Quantity)

A dynamical system consists of objects that effect and are effected by other objects. Each object is constituted by its energy, but for the object to have a real limit in space, it must also be determined by the energies of other objects. The quantity of energy necessary for the effecting object to have an effect is its Force, whereas the motion of the object effected is its Velocity.

## Momentum and Displacement (Quantum)

While the forces and velocities of a dynamical system are the conditions which determine its equations of motion, force and velocity are themselves only a momentary rates of change. Force is the rate of Momentum and velocity is the rate of Displacement. What moves has momentum insofar as it has been forced by another and what is moved is displaced insofar as it has a velocity or speed. 

## Action (Ratio)

The object and the path that it moves along both have spatiotemporal extension. But what solicits the object in motion along its path of motion is another object that is moving along its own path of motion. This series of causes does not proceed endlessly since the motion of the whole is the One Cause of the motions of its parts determined as the Effects of the whole. This One Cause of motion is Gravity, of which all particular systems of motion are its Effects. Each system of motion, therefore, is necessarily relative to the apparent motions of other systems.
