# Sublation

Since the concepts that are revealed by the speculative method are determinations of thought, this logically implies a thinking being that is comprehending said concepts by their own engagement in the method. Therefore, the reader may think that it really was necessary all along conclude that the thinking being’s own consciousness or “I” is what is driving this whole process of Reason in the first place. This still replicates Kant’s position that Reason is only instantiated or present in thinking beings, but not reality as a whole. Simply because concepts are thinkable does not mean that their rationality is contingent upon whether or not we think them. In fact, in thinking about them we do not imaginatively develop what Reason is, rather in thinking we are witnessing the development of Reason itself. 

Reason’s task is to explicate the deficiency of concepts generated by The Understanding as necessarily coming into contradiction with its opposite (dialectical) and deriving from this a successive concept which unifies and includes the preceding ones as moments (speculative). This process of determining a more comprehensive concept through the inclusion of prior concepts is called sublation. Translated from the German verb “Aufheben”, sublation simultaneously means “to cancel”, “to preserve” and “to elevate”. What is cancelled in sublation is the universality that the prior concept and its opposite simultaneously claimed to be, which is what brought them into contradiction in the first place. If they are shown to be just moments of an all-encompassing concept, then neither of which are universal after all. What is preserved in sublation is the meaning of each concept in their identity and distinction. Each concept and its opposite form a negative unity as moments of a totality. And what is elevated in sublation is the generation of a new successive concept that is a logical consequence of the concepts preceding it.

The last instance of sublation is the presuppositionless system of philosophy as a whole - the Absolute Idea. The unconditioned universal which includes and encompasses all rational concepts. It is completely descriptively self-contained, so there are no rational concepts left out nor any concepts which has not been derived as a necessary step in the derivation of the system as a whole.
