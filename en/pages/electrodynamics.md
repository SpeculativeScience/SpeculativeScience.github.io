## Light

### Source of Light

In gravity, all matter is drawn to the simple unity of the center, although it also radiates out into the infinite multiplicity of the periphery. This distribution of matter in space, brought about by the balance of attraction and repulsion, occurs in the most direct way in the cosmic nebula. As the web enveloping all matter, it itself seems hardly heavy, but only to present the dark fluid in which gravity takes place. The cosmic nebula has already attained the identity of matter, toward which everything within it, without reaching it, merely strives through gravity. But it is this identity only as dark matter, or as the mere possibility from which light becomes actual. The true source of light is the star, because in it, more correctly, the source itself has become form, and the form itself the source. As the first concentration of the loose nebula into a more solid core, it takes the unity of gravity seriously by truly concentrating the spatial separation of many points of the cosmic nebula into one point, disproving the impermeability of matter, and thus creating a real identity of form within it. The more nebula is united in one point, the more intense this identity will be, but the more intense the light is, the more extensive it can also become without being lost in the darkness of the nebula, and this is what we call luminescence. The star is therefore primarily called the self-luminous body, and is nothing other than this. It has eliminated all darkness, all multiplicity, all materiality in the immanent point of unity of its core. It is, in a sense, the pure form of identity without further matter, just as the cosmic nebula is the mere matter of identity, which still lacks its own form, but only in a relative way, since no aspect can be entirely missing.

### Emission of Light

Light is the existing identity as such, in that it does not remain with the cosmic nebula's striving for unity, as the mere possibility of unity, but rather, as activity and energy, attains actual unity. Since the dark matter itself is nothing other than the cosmic nebula, the real concept of light, as the cosmic nebula set in action, which is not as matter, but as the form-activity of matter. The source of light is therefore the activity of matter itself. Since activity as such also forms the source of thought, the question has been raised whether light is even matter or thought. And this question can be answered by saying that, compared to gravity as the material point of unity, it is rather the immaterial unity, within nature itself, so light is the immaterial materiality.

## Electromagnetism

### Magnetism

The activity of spatial forming can, according to the concept of magnetism, be no other than that the stationary points, which, as drops, are the material of form, set themselves in motion, go beyond themselves: negate themselves, and through this negation produce other points. The points set in motion, however, are the lines. The mechanical formative process is thus the progression from point to line. The activity of forming includes linearity within itself, whereas punctuality was merely potential. However, the line cannot produce a form if it moves only in one and the same direction; even less can the point and the sphere, which are still completely directionless. The activity of forming therefore presupposes opposite directions of the lines. But if these lines, from their opposition, were to come to unity, to join together, the plane and the enclosing surface would emerge. This would be the total, the true mechanical form. Therefore, if the pure activity of form is to be maintained in its rigor, the lines cannot come to a connection, to rest, but must remain in constant unrest.

### Electricity

Since magnetism is the formative activity that seeks to unite the spatial dimensions into a total mechanical shape, electricity is the effort to elevate the physical differences of form to the totality of the body. Yet, like magnetism, it remains at the level of mere activity without arriving at the real product. Since the differences are not yet truly resolved into unity, the latter confronts the independence of the former and, as a merely abstract unity, falls beyond the differences, just as the latter falls beyond it. This is the contradiction of electricity. Because the differences are no longer ideal boundaries, as mere formative elements of space, as in magnetism, but always already real corporealities, the poles are no longer, as lines, held in a purely mathematical way in the unity of the point of indifference, which was the only real thing. Instead, in electricity, the poles are now much more the only real thing, they are distributed among two different bodies, and their unity is the purely ideal bond. Magnetism and electricity thus represent the logical opposition of identity and opposition, of ideality and reality. Since in electricity, the different corporeality, as such, not yet total individuality, is active in three dimensions, and unity, as an actual body, is equally absent, electricity acts only as a surface or with two dimensions. 

Secondly, if we move on to the objects of electrical activity, these can only be the stated differences of form itself: namely, those of neutrality and combustibility. The tension into which these two qualities enter in order to cancel out their one-sidedness against each other was originally observed in glass and resin. But soon the domain of electricity expanded, as a multitude of similar, different bodies exhibited the same potentials. Even the most superficial differences, such as the roughness and smoothness of the same material, were sufficient to produce the same electrical potential. And so one arrived from the sensible difference to the conceptual difference between positive and negative charge. As the differences became potentialized, as properties of the total thing, became one-sided things: so the form-activity, as the differentiating element in these materials, is merely the other one-sided moment of the totality, which stands opposite to the material.
